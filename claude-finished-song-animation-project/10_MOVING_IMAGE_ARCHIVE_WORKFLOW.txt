MOVING IMAGE ARCHIVE — REQUIRED PER-SONG FOOTAGE WORKFLOW

PURPOSE AND SOURCE
Use the Moving Image Archive as a real archival-footage source in every finished-song production, not only as mood-board inspiration:
- Song-specific search: https://www.movingimagearchive.com/#search
- About and reuse statement: https://www.movingimagearchive.com/about

Use the archive's search page for song-specific themes and shot needs. The archive describes itself as a searchable library drawn from public-domain collections and says its material is free to reuse. The user-supplied gallery screenshot (2026-10-25) showed natural-language “Describe a shot” search, individual clip previews/downloads, and filters for color, year and aspect ratio; it displayed 2,280 films and 122,218 clips. These counts and controls may change. Search by the shot, preview the returned clip, and download only selected clips. Do not mirror or download the whole archive.

This is an external footage library, separate from local code repositories R01–R16. At each song’s start, scan/rank all 16 catalogued repositories and record the evidence in `productions/<song>/research/REPOSITORY_REVIEW.md` per `13_REPOSITORY_NAVIGATION_AND_USE.txt`. Use suitable repositories for techniques; use this guide for archival footage. R12 is an AI-video prompt catalog, not this footage archive. Repository ranking and archive-footage selection are related discovery tasks, but neither makes a source an animation engine.

PRELINGER / INTERNET ARCHIVE DISCOVERY
For the Internet Archive footage requirement, search Prelinger Archives Online at https://prelingerarchives.online/ when a historical film, home movie, industrial, educational, government or sponsored-film treatment fits the song. Its search page is the Prelinger-specific discovery interface; inspect the result and preview it, then use the linked Internet Archive item record for file metadata, item-level rights and download. The shared API helper can search the Internet Archive Prelinger collection with `--collection prelinger`; it does not query the Prelinger site itself. These are two discovery routes to Prelinger material within the Internet Archive, not two separate footage providers. Treat each item's rights independently and do not infer public-domain status from its collection membership.

REQUIRED ADDITIONAL FOOTAGE SOURCES
Every finished song must include at least one relevant, reviewed Moving Image Archive clip plus at least one suitable, rights-cleared clip from Wikimedia Commons and at least one from Internet Archive. Prelinger is an Internet Archive collection and may supply the IA clip when a relevant item exists. Search and document each source separately; none substitutes for another. Follow `15_MEDIA_API_DISCOVERY_WORKFLOW.txt` and `shared-animation-library/media-discovery/README.md` for the API searches, rights gate and separate provenance manifests. Public search and metadata inspection need no OAuth. Commons requires a truthful contact URL/email in the API User-Agent; signing in to a Claude browser does not provide that value to the local Node CLI. Do not force irrelevant footage or bypass the rights gate. If a source cannot provide a suitable clip, record its requirement unmet and do not call the reel finished. The helper’s rights allowlist is a cautious screen, not a legal opinion. Keep Internet Archive/Prelinger separate from the Moving Image Archive and R10’s film-study repo.

REQUIRED SONG-SPECIFIC PROCESS
1. Establish this song’s recording, lyrics, brief, narrative, measured duration, native 4:5 or 9:16 frame and intended visual treatment. Keep all searches and decisions inside this song’s conversation and `productions/<song>/` folder.
2. Before locking the storyboard, define each planned footage moment as a visual target: visible subject, action, setting, helpful shot scale/viewpoint, and space needed for code action, captions or crop. Translate lyric meaning into observable imagery; do not search only exact lyric words or abstract moods.
3. Build a small query ladder for important moments: (a) direct subject + action + setting; (b) historically/geographically grounded wording when supported by the song; and (c) a visual-adjacent alternative that preserves the scene meaning without pretending to be literal. Keep each query focused on one scene idea. Vary one concept at a time so you can tell what improves results. Search broad enough to discover unexpected material, then apply year, color/B&W and aspect-ratio filters deliberately. Avoid stacking constraints that could hide useful clips.
4. Preview actual returned clips, not only titles or thumbnails. Check the relevant source interval for subject, action, context, framing, image condition and usable edit points. Separate what the footage visibly shows from catalog description, historical context and your interpretation. If a clip is associative rather than literal, say so in the plan; do not imply an identity, event or action the image does not establish. When a direct query fails, broaden or change one term, try an adjacent visual target, and record if no suitable result remains.
5. Compare a short list against the visual target. Give concise 1–5 evidence-based ratings for direct visual match, useful action/readability, fit to the chosen frame and code/caption layout, contextual fit, and technical usability. The ratings organize judgment; they are not objective archive scores. Search rank is only a discovery hint. Keep rights/reuse review as a separate gate, not a ranking bonus. Prefer a clip that serves the scene clearly and leaves room for the code-built visual story.
6. Save the exact search trail in `productions/<song>/research/MIA_SEARCH_LOG.md`, using `repository-preparation/templates/MIA_SEARCH_LOG_TEMPLATE.md`: verbatim query, date, filters, result count, saved result path, candidates actually previewed, selection/rejection reason and the evidence behind each description. Keep a useful shortlist rather than copying every result. Record selected clips in `productions/<song>/archive/CLIP_MANIFEST.md` (or equivalent structured file): item/clip ID and title; item URL; displayed year and source/collection; archive reuse statement and item-specific rights note; search-log reference; original clip duration and chosen source in/out; downloaded filename and SHA-256; codec, dimensions, frame rate and audio-track presence when measured; scene ID; production timeline in/out; intended crop/fit; attribution text; and whether source audio is muted. Keep the original download intact and put a production copy under that song’s `public/archive/` folder. Never reuse another song’s clip by default.
7. Select and download only suitable individual clips. Every finished song must include at least one actual Moving Image Archive clip in its final rendered reel. Use more when they support the story; do not force footage into every scene or let it replace the song’s original code-art direction. If reasonable searches yield no suitable clip, document queries/results and flag the unmet requirement rather than silently substituting another source or delivering without archival footage.

## SEARCH PRACTICE LEARNED FROM I AM PROMETHEAN
The saved Claude production contains 13 topic-named files under `productions/i-am-promethean-comparison/claude/archive/mia-search/`, each holding 24 returned candidates and fields such as clip/item ID, title, source interval, duration, aspect, color mode and search score. The manifest traces selected footage to scene beats and local source intervals; saved item pages and `mia-pages/clips.tsv` provide additional selection/provenance evidence. The search set covers distinct visual needs such as woodland, classroom, doctor, moon, aircraft, ranch, seaside, law and baking. This is useful evidence that scene-specific visual targets can produce a varied, traceable shortlist.

The exact natural-language query strings, filters and rejected-candidate reasoning were not saved alongside those result JSON files. Topic filenames do not prove the exact query. Future runs must close that gap with the search-log template; do not retrofit guessed query text into this historical case. The selected footage, quilt treatment, palette, framing and narrative remain specific to I Am Promethean. The reusable method is the traceable path: scene visual target → query variants → actual preview → ranked shortlist → scene caller.

RIGHTS AND PROVENANCE
The archive’s published free-to-reuse statement is the basis for sourcing its archival footage. Preserve the exact item page, source/collection attribution and any rights information displayed for the chosen clip. Do not infer additional rights to an embedded soundtrack, narration, trademarks or separately credited materials. Mute or omit archival audio by default so the supplied song master remains the only soundtrack. If item details disclose a restriction or conflicting provenance, choose another item or resolve the issue before use. Do not describe the archive’s general statement as a new license grant from GPT or from the user.

REMOTION IMPLEMENTATION
Keep the complete production in the connected kit. Place selected media in that song’s `public/archive/` and declare each clip in its `PROJECT.assets` using a path relative to the production folder and the existing generic media-asset path. Remotion scenes receive resolved asset URLs through their `assets` prop. Use the installed Remotion 4.0.538 APIs only; `OffthreadVideo` is available from `remotion` for frame-accurate video rendering and uses FFmpeg to extract source frames. Use documented frame-based trimming and timing for the installed version. Do not install a second Remotion package or create another video project.

Compose each clip within the selected 4:5 or 9:16 frame from the start. Preserve its aspect ratio; choose crop, contain, split-frame or layered treatment deliberately for the specific shot. Do not distort footage or use a crop that removes its essential action. Code-authored scene design, typography, masks, texture, camera treatment or overlays should give the clip a clear role in the song’s visual arc. Keep archive source sound muted unless the user makes a separate song-specific audio decision.

CHECKS AND QUALITY REVIEW
- Run the shared no-media `--plan` before spending render time. It can validate config and paths; it does not prove a video decodes, appears correctly or is visually good.
- For pre-export scene samples, use the shared Remotion `--stills` route. After an authorized complete candidate is rendered, use `11_HEADLESS_BROWSER_FRAME_REVIEW.txt` to have Playwright decode and capture selected MP4 frames, including the archival clip passages, and FFmpeg build a contact sheet. Preserve the command, evidence and exit status. A page load or successful frame probe is not visual acceptance.
- Use installed FFmpeg to inspect selected source media (duration, dimensions, frame rate, codec, audio tracks) and make compact contact sheets from deliberate sample times for source and candidate review. Contact sheets reduce review cost; they do not establish smooth motion, pacing, easing, transition quality, lyric sync or a full viewing.
- Include every selected archival treatment in the risk-based still set: first/representative/last use, crop or masked edge, caption overlap, color or image-quality risk, and cut/transition where relevant. Inspect stills at full resolution and phone size. After full-candidate playback is authorized, watch the entire reel with the supplied master and check transitions into/out of footage and any perceived sync. Distinguish technical decode, browser readiness, still appearance, motion review, actual audio sync and user acceptance.
- Use the finite correction rounds in `07_QUALITY_REVIEW_AND_ACCEPTANCE.txt`. Do not render a full video for every local still-only adjustment; do render a complete new candidate when changed motion, clip timing or integration needs full review.

AUTHORIZATION AND HANDOFF
A single user prompt may authorize archive search, preview, downloading selected free-to-reuse clips, implementation, browser checks, still review and complete-song rendering. If that prompt authorizes those stages, carry them through without asking for the same routine approval again. A planning-only prompt stops before download, implementation or media output. Do not purchase clips, create accounts, submit credentials, install packages or access paid footage without separate authorization. Save the manifest, clip source files, exact commands, checks, review evidence and checkpoint within the named song folder. Keep any final output and its archive provenance together.

LIMITS
Search results, metadata and contact sheets help select footage but do not prove every historical detail, image quality, smooth playback or rights for material outside the archive’s stated scope. Verify each selected file and its provenance locally. The installed Remotion route’s archival-video decode, crop, timing and final audio/video behavior must be established for the actual song through its authorized checks; do not claim those passed from this guide alone.
